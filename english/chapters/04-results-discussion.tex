\chapter{Results and Discussion}\label{chap:results}

This thesis shows that sparsity-robust, contingency-table statistics yield positive results for recovering \gls{dtu} from 3'-biased single-cell RNA-seq data. Applied to both a human cell line mixture and mouse cortical tissue, the adapted framework identified biologically consistent, mutually exclusive isoform switches, including TPM1, Meg3, and Malat1.

However, the analysis does not jump straight to these core findings. Because the dataset relies on highly sparse terminal counts, every DTU call is only as trustworthy as the quality and clustering checks beneath it. Therefore, this chapter is structured as a fixed sequence of checks, with each phase guarding the step that follows. It first states the dimensions of the filtered matrices that bound every downstream step (Section~\ref{sec:matrix-dimensions}), then evaluates the \gls{gdi} to confirm that the raw transcript counts carry enough differential signal to separate distinct cell populations (Section~\ref{sec:gdi-transcript}). It then compares clustering partitions across gene and transcript matrices to locate exactly where isoform information alters the cellular grouping (Section~\ref{sec:clustering-comparison}). Only after these foundational tests validate the matrix's integrity does the analysis proceed to extract the DTU candidates (Section~\ref{sec:dtu-results}) and stress-test them against contrast thresholds (Section~\ref{sec:tau-sweep}) and the published literature (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Dataset Quality Assessment via GDI}

Before DTU calls can be interpreted, the matrix itself must be shown to carry enough variability to distinguish cell populations. The \gls{gdi}, defined in Section~\ref{sec:cotan-framework}, provides this check without any clustering: it measures how much each feature varies across cells, so constitutive features sit near the null median (about $1.305$) while features that mark distinct populations rise above the threshold. The question here is whether the transcript-level counts still separate the populations once the gene-level resolution is replaced by sparse isoform counts.

\subsection*{Transcript-level GDI Distribution}\label{sec:gdi-transcript}

Figure~\ref{fig:gditranscript} plots the GDI of the mouse cortex dataset computed on transcript counts. Each point represents a single transcript, with the x-axis showing log-scaled average expression and the y-axis showing the GDI value. Highlighted labels track specific transcripts belonging to known housekeeping (HK), neural-progenitor (NPG), and post-mitotic-neuron (PNG) marker genes.

Crucially, the transcript-level distribution upends classical gene-level expectations, revealing the mechanical realities of isoform quantification:

\textbf{The Minor Isoform Collapse:} Several transcripts belonging to established NPG and PNG marker genes sit unexpectedly low, hovering near the blue null median line of roughly $1.305$. While the parent genes strongly identify cell types in aggregate, their minor or unexpressed isoforms fall victim to extreme sparsity. Without sufficient detection density to generate significant co-expression $p$-values, these specific variants flatline. This confirms that a marker gene's predictive power does not automatically transfer to all its constituent transcripts.

\textbf{The Housekeeping Variance Spike:} Conversely, several HK transcripts break well above the red target threshold of $1.5$. Because housekeeping genes are universally highly expressed, they generate massive quantities of 3'-biased terminal reads. The pseudo-aligner's Expectation-Maximization (EM) algorithm must distribute these ambiguous reads across overlapping equivalence classes. This process can introduce highly variable, cluster-correlated fractional assignments that COTAN correctly flags as structural variance, exposing either an EM quantification artifact or a genuine, unexpected isoform switch within a constitutive gene.

\textbf{Decoupling Gene from Transcript:} The presence of a broad cloud of transcripts rising well above the threshold confirms that the sparse matrix retains robust differential signal. However, the inversion of the highlighted markers demonstrates that transcript-level variation is fundamentally decoupled from parent-gene identity, validating the necessity of analyzing these features as independent statistical entities.

\subsection*{Gene vs Transcript GDI Comparison}

Figure~\ref{fig:gdi_genes} repeats the GDI computation on the standard gene-level matrix, highlighting the identical housekeeping and marker categories. Because the gene run utilizes the denser, independent GEO matrix (28,692 features against the transcript matrix's 12,848), it serves as a baseline for how these markers behave when isoform-level sparsity is removed.

Comparing this gene-level distribution to the transcript-level plot reveals the profound impact of aggregation:

\textbf{Recovery of Marker Signal:} At the gene level, the expected biological hierarchy is restored. Neural-progenitor (NPG) and post-mitotic-neuron (PNG) markers---such as Map2, Hes5, and Vim---clearly detach from the baseline cloud and ascend to high GDI values (2.0 to 4.0). By summing the reads of all constituent isoforms, the matrix recovers the statistical density required for COTAN to easily separate these distinct cellular populations.

\textbf{Physiological Variance in Housekeeping Genes:} While several housekeeping genes (Amacr, Tars) anchor the baseline as expected, others (Zfr, Calm1, Actb) exhibit surprisingly high GDI scores. At the aggregated gene level, this spread reflects genuine biological variance. Because housekeeping genes are universally and highly expressed, their read counts are sensitive to systemic cellular differences---such as total library size, metabolic activity, and cell cycle stage---that naturally differ between a differentiating progenitor and a mature neuron. COTAN accurately captures this broad physiological variance as statistical non-independence.

Ultimately, these two GDI analyses establish a critical foundation for the DTU pipeline: while gene-level aggregation is required to achieve the statistical power necessary to validate canonical cell-type markers, the transcript-level matrix still preserves sufficient differential signal to distinguish populations, validating the search for isoform-specific switches.

\section{Clustering Comparison: Gene vs Transcript Level}\label{sec:clustering-comparison}

To quantify how gene and transcript clustering diverge, a confusion matrix was constructed across the 4,077 common cells present in both analyses. Figure~\ref{fig:confusion-matrix} cross-tabulates the 11 transcript-level clusters against the 15 gene-level clusters, revealing three distinct structural patterns that justify the isoform-level resolution.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{heatmap_genes_vs_transcripts.pdf}
    \caption{Confusion matrix comparing transcript-level (11 clusters) and gene-level (15 clusters) assignments for the 4,077 common cells of the mouse dataset.}
    \label{fig:confusion-matrix}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Diagonal dominance (signal preservation):} The majority of cells receive consistent assignments across both methods, grouping into heavy blocks along the diagonal (e.g., 261 cells cleanly mapping Gene cluster 04 to Transcript cluster 02). This validates that the transcript-level pipeline does not produce random partitions: despite the extreme sparsity of the filtered transcript matrix (12,848 features versus 28,692 genes), it recovers the broad cellular architecture without being overwhelmed by technical dropouts.

\textbf{Systematic splitting (hidden sub-states):} Several broad gene-level clusters break down into finer sub-regions at the transcript level. For example, Gene cluster 15 splits cleanly into Transcript clusters 07 (179 cells) and 08 (144 cells); similarly, the massive Gene cluster 12 distributes its cells predominantly across Transcript clusters 09, 10, and 11. This divergence suggests that populations appearing homogeneous under aggregated gene expression actually contain distinct sub-groups utilising fundamentally different isoform programs.

\textbf{Selective merging (shared splicing profiles):} Conversely, the transcript matrix collapses some distinctions enforced by the gene matrix. Transcript cluster 04 merges populations from Gene clusters 05 (346 cells) and 06 (123 cells) into a single group. Cells differing in overall transcriptional activity could nonetheless share a unified, dominant isoform-usage profile.

The discrepancy in total cluster counts (11 versus 15) is not a simple resolution ranking but a reflection of the matrices themselves: the transcript matrix is a heavily feature-filtered view, whereas the gene matrix is a denser reference count set. Ultimately, the confusion matrix demonstrates agreement in the large and divergence in the particular: the local splits and merges mark precisely where isoform information redraws biological boundaries, exposing the exact regime where downstream DTU detection---such as the transcript-exclusive Srrm2 and Grin2b switches---adds biological value over standard gene-centric workflows.

However, this raises a critical question: whether the detected DTU events hold across clustering strategies, or depend entirely on how cells are partitioned. The robustness analysis below answers it.

\section{Core Finding: DTU Detection Results}\label{sec:dtu-results}
The algorithm from Section~\ref{sec:methods-dtu-algorithm} was applied to both human and mouse datasets, filtering for mutually exclusive isoform pairs with bidirectional cluster preference (threshold $\tau$ as specified). The results show that contingency-table statistics can recover within-gene exclusivity patterns even in sparse 3'-biased data.

\subsection*{Human Dataset Findings (Arigoni)}\label{sec:results-human}

The human cell line mixture provided ground-truth validation through known biology. Roughly a tenth of the genes carrying at least two detected transcripts retain at least one significant mutually exclusive pair, yet after the bidirectional contrast threshold $\tau = 0.2$ only a handful of candidates survive, across just a few genes (Table~\ref{tab:dtu-funnel})\footnote{Counted by \texttt{extract\_dtu\_candidates()} and recorded in the run log (\texttt{results/arrigoni/logs/}); the exported \texttt{dtu\_candidates.csv} holds only the survivors.}. That collapse is the algorithm working as designed: mutual exclusivity alone admits hundreds of pairs, but only a handful also flip their enrichment between clusters, and it is this second condition---a pair must be preferred in one cell type and disfavoured in another---that separates a genuine switch from a statistical curiosity.

\subsection*{Mouse Dataset Findings (Ding)}\label{sec:results-mouse}

For mouse brain tissue a lower threshold, $\tau = 0.09$, was applied, and DTU extraction was run on both clusterizations---the transcript-derived partition and the gene-derived one. Both runs start from the same funnel---14 genes carrying 53 significant pairs at the loosest point ($\tau = 0.01$)---but diverge once the contrast is applied: 41 candidates across 5 genes survive at $\tau = 0.09$ in the transcript-derived clusters, and 38 across 3 genes in the gene-derived ones (Table~\ref{tab:dtu-funnel-mouse}). The inversion is itself informative---fewer initial pairs but more survivors means mouse cortex switches are gradual rather than sharp, so a relaxed threshold admits many weak but real transitions that the strict human cutoff would have discarded. The gene-derived set is a strict subset of the transcript-derived one: all 38 pairs it detects are also found by the transcript partition, which additionally captures three---Grin2b and two Srrm2 switches---exclusive to it.

\subsection*{Threshold Sensitivity: The Tau Sweep}\label{sec:tau-sweep}

The candidate count fundamentally depends on the differential expression contrast threshold ($\tau$). Because this parameter was initially selected pragmatically to yield a biologically manageable candidate list (on the order of tens of events) rather than derived from a formal statistical null distribution, it is critical to verify that the chosen values do not sit on a fragile algorithmic edge. To evaluate this stability, the DTU extraction was re-run over a fine grid of $\tau$ values (ranging from 0.005 to 0.300 at intervals of 0.005), recording the number of accepted events and unique genes at each step.

The resulting sensitivity sweep acts as an internal consistency check, confirming that the event count decreases monotonically as the threshold strictly increases.

\textbf{Human Dataset Dynamics (Figures~\ref{fig:tau-sweep} and~\ref{fig:tau-sweep-zoom}):} Figure~\ref{fig:tau-sweep} maps this threshold landscape for the homogeneous human cell-line mixture. The bulk of significant pairs descends in a smooth, continuous curve---dropping from 2006 events at $\tau = 0.005$ to 21 events at $\tau = 0.2$. This nearly featureless gradient reflects the nature of the well-separated cell lines: almost every isoform pair carries some reciprocal contrast, creating a dense spectrum with no natural break.

On such a continuous slope, any single threshold might appear arbitrary. However, as shown in the zoomed-in view of Figure~\ref{fig:tau-sweep-zoom}, a genuine plateau emerges at $\tau = 0.25$, where the candidate count holds perfectly flat at ten events through $\tau = 0.26$. The published threshold of $\tau = 0.2$, which successfully reproduces the 21 reported candidates, sits immediately at the foot of this slope, just below the stable highland. Therefore, the human result is anchored at the boundary of a stable topological region rather than lost arbitrarily on a volatile tail.

\textbf{Mouse Dataset Dynamics:} The threshold landscape for the mouse cortex behaves structurally differently. Rather than a smooth gradient, the candidate count descends in discrete steps, with the 41 events at $\tau = 0.09$ holding steady across the wide band $\tau = 0.07$--$0.09$ before stepping down through 40, 39, and 38 (Figure~\ref{fig:tau-sweep-mouse}). Because the heterogeneous tissue is dominated by a few high-contrast isoform switches accompanied by a highly sparse tail, its stability regions manifest as broad ledges rather than a single distinct summit. The gene-derived clusters trace a similar shape but reach extinction sooner, holding ledges at 38 ($\tau = 0.09$--$0.12$) and 37 ($\tau = 0.13$--$0.14$) before collapsing (Figure~\ref{fig:tau-sweep-mouse-gene}). The chosen threshold of $\tau = 0.09$ is the point where both partitions sit simultaneously on their plateaus---the joint-stable point that makes the call principled rather than arbitrary.

Ultimately, this parameter sweep provides a vital post-hoc anchor for the heuristic thresholds. While the selected values (0.2 for human, 0.09 for mouse) were originally targeted for validation feasibility, the sweep proves they reside within or directly adjacent to mathematically stable regions. Consequently, the final candidate lists are robust structural findings, not artifacts of a single, fragile threshold value. However, this sensitivity sweep remains a diagnostic heuristic; establishing a true permutation-based null distribution would be the optimal future methodology to provide statistically calibrated $p$-values for the contrast metric.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection*{Key Findings and Literature Validation}

The top candidates (Meg3 \cite{uroda2019meg3}, Malat1 \cite{ghanam2023malat1}, TPM1 \cite{xue2012specialized}) align with documented biology---lncRNA switching in neurogenesis and ribosomal protein regulation---which indicates that the algorithm recovers genuine signal rather than technical artifacts. Convergence across a human cell line mixture and mouse brain tissue further indicates that the method holds under different biological contexts rather than a single favourable case study. Table~\ref{tab:dtu-validation} frames these calls along a gradient from strong evidence to uncharted ground, and reading the results along that gradient both validates the method and marks what it cannot yet claim.

    \centering
    {\small
    \begin{tabular}{@{}p{2.6cm} p{3.4cm} p{3.4cm} p{3.4cm}@{}}
        \toprule
        Dataset & Strong evidence & Plausible, unconfirmed & Treat with caution \\
        \midrule
        Human / NSCLC lines & TPM1, RPS24, SAT1 & DSTN, TPM4, SNHG5 & RPL10, RPL35A, RPS8 \\
        Mouse / cortex & Meg3, Malat1 & Pde10a, Grin2b, Srrm2 & --- \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}}
    \caption{Literature support for the DTU candidate genes. Strong-evidence genes are directly documented in prior isoform studies; plausible genes are consistent with related biology but not directly confirmed; caution genes lack prior DTU literature and need orthogonal validation.}
    \label{tab:dtu-validation}
\end{table}

\textbf{Strong evidence:} The strongest candidates (TPM1, Meg3, Malat1) are consistent with biology reported in prior isoform studies, but this thesis makes no biological claims beyond that consistency. TPM1 is a cell-type-specific tropomyosin switch, and Meg3 and Malat1 are long non-coding RNAs\defn{Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA)}{A long RNA molecule that does not encode a protein but regulates gene expression, splicing, and development.} whose alternative isoform usage is documented \cite{uroda2019meg3, ghanam2023malat1, xue2012specialized}. What the detector actually reports is a single, well-defined pattern: one gene carrying two isoforms that are used in opposite directions in different cell clusters---a reciprocal, mutually exclusive switch. The same pattern recurs across genes with no shared mechanism, and this convergence is the signal that the detector captures structure rather than noise. The interpretation is given in terms of discrete cluster preferences rather than a differentiation trajectory, since the original study states that its samples were not appropriate for pseudotime analysis \cite{ding2020systematic}.

\textbf{Plausible but unconfirmed:} The middle tier (DSTN, TPM4, SNHG5; Pde10a, Grin2b, Srrm2) presents biologically consistent patterns of alternative usage that align with related literature, though none is directly confirmed as an isoform switch in prior studies. These calls extend the method's reach without yet anchoring it. On the mouse side, Pde10a is detected by both clusterizations, while Grin2b and Srrm2 surface only in the transcript-derived partition at contrast just above the $\tau = 0.09$ bar, so they sit at the boundary between plausible and confirmed.

\textbf{Treated with caution:} The final tier (RPL10, RPL35A, RPS8 in the human dataset) carries significant statistical switching in this matrix but no prior DTU literature. These novel candidates are exactly the calls that require orthogonal validation: confirming them would demand an orthogonal experiment such as quantitative PCR\defn{RT-PCR}{Reverse-transcription polymerase chain reaction: a lab technique that experimentally confirms whether a specific isoform is present, used as an orthogonal check on computational DTU calls.} or long-read sequencing, or a re-run on an independent dataset to see whether the same isoform switches recur. Binding validation to this tier turns the absence of ground truth into a concrete next step rather than a detached caveat. The mouse cortex retains no caution-tier candidates at the raised threshold: the weak, unanchored calls (Ttc14, Zfp941, Snrnp70, Lrrc7) all fall below the $\tau = 0.09$ contrast bar and are dropped, which is itself the precision gain of the stricter threshold.

A further conclusion concerns clustering resolution. The strongest signals survive both clusterizations---they are cluster-agnostic and validate the core functionality---while the subtler switches surface only in the transcript-derived partition: Grin2b and two Srrm2 events are captured by the transcript clusters but absent from the gene-derived set, which at $\tau = 0.09$ is a strict subset of the transcript one. The choice of partition therefore shapes which biology becomes visible: the strongest signals are robust, but fine-grained isoform transitions reveal themselves only under the isoform-aware resolution.

\subsection*{Technical Limitations}

The constraints that temper these results are grouped under one heading:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{3'-bias invisibility}: Alternative splicing events occurring in transcript mid-regions remain undetectable when only \gls{threePrimeEnd}s are sequenced. The DTU candidates found here represent a subset---those where isoforms differ at their terminal exons or 3' ends.

    \item \textbf{EM rounding effects}: Fractional EM assignments were rounded to integers for COTAN compatibility (Section~\ref{sec:data-filtering}). Low-abundance isoforms with small fractional counts may be distorted by this discretization, potentially introducing false negatives or altered contrast values.

    \item \textbf{Threshold sensitivity}: The choice of $\tau$ lacks formal statistical grounding. The tau sweep (Section~\ref{sec:tau-sweep}) anchors the threshold empirically at a stable plateau, but a permutation-based null distribution would still provide calibrated $p$-values rather than a consistency-based choice.
\end{itemize}
